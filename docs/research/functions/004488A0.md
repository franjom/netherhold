Read 0x004488A0-0x00448A51 in the disassembly, for the draw-bit writers sweep of the possession-hide question (it has nothing to do with possession as far as read). The record's tile is its first dword (low word x, high word y); the player word at +8 is compared with the view's own player (vtable +0xBC, falling back to the world's local player at vtable +0x264) and a flag at +4 decides. The cell comes from view slot +0x1D8, and the thing from 0x00453670(cell, 4, &out). A record for the viewer's own player forces the flag to zero (0x004488FA). With the flag zero it sets bit 0 of thing +0xF3 and of the thing named by its word +0x101; otherwise it clears both, so the thing is shown to its own player and hidden from the other viewers. The thing's class and what the definition bit 0x20 means (a door or trap kind) are not tied to a name, and the sender of the record is UNREAD beyond one: the reveal arm of 0x004A55F0 posts a record of this layout (tile, 0, the door's owner) as command 0x11 at 0x004A65C4..0x004A6604. That table entry 0x0066D154 is the handler of command 0x11 is not checked against the table's base.

The flag (EDX at 0x00448945) is also what 0x00443590 writes into bit 15 of the tile's presentation cell at 0x004489E7..0x004489EC, before the 3 by 3 redraw: the tile is drawn as wall for a viewer the door is hidden from (record 004434F0). The class-4 thing with definition bit 0x20 is a Secret Door (`DoorFlags.Secret`); the child at +0x101 is its lock object.
