| Q16 | MAP tile bytes 3 (`flags`, bridge-related) and 4 — meaning? | **BYTE 3 ANSWERED** (2026-09-03, ADR-0031): it marks **a bridge over lava** — 1 everywhere in all 66 shipped maps, 2 on bridge tiles over lava and nowhere else. The old note that it was "bridge-related" was right and it was the whole meaning. See Q64 for the evidence and `BridgeRules` for the reading; whether it carries any *further* meaning on non-bridge terrain is still unknown, but it takes no third value anywhere so there is nothing left to mean. **BYTE 4 is "unused"** — CONFIRMED by an independent source (2013 KeeperKlan reversal thread, quoted in `external-resources.md`), which documents the element as `{BYTE idTerrain; BYTE idPlayer; BYTE flags; BYTE unused;}` and says of byte 3 that "only a '2' bit here is interpreted to do anything special at load". That corroborates all three of our findings from a direction with no stake in them — the field is flags, 2 is the only meaningful value, and byte 4 is unused — and what the 2 was *for* was open there, which is what our 66-map sweep supplied. Our own evidence for byte 4 was that it is **uniform across an entire level** — every tile of Level6b carries 1 and every tile of Level20 carries 0 — so it is a level property rather than tile data, and it does not correlate with whether the level has water or lava. A map format version or a level flag |
| Q17 | MAP `playerId` numbering: which id is neutral? (level1: p1=32, p2=1048, p3=60) | **ANSWERED** (2026-09-04, ADR-0043, `PlayerIds`): **2 is neutral**, 0 is unowned terrain, 1 is the hero side, 3–7 are keepers. Confirmed three ways, only one of which is OpenKeeper's `Player` constants — across all 66 levels every dungeon-heart tile belongs to a player between 3 and 7, and so does every creature pool and creature-availability entry. | Q18 | `maxHealthTypeTerrainId` on Claimed Path points at Rock — does not fit the "upgrade at full health" reading. Second meaning for path tiles? | **ANSWERED** (2026-08-29) — no second meaning. Claimed Path's upward job is fill-in, whose result is rock. Evidence: the `FillInable` flag falls on exactly the five floor types, and six terrain types ship at full health pointing elsewhere, which refutes the level-triggered reading outright.
