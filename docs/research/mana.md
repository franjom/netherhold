# Mana, and why it is the size of a dungeon

Milestone M3. Where a keeper's mana comes from, what stops it growing, and what
drains it.

**Implementation:** `DK2.Simulation.ManaRules` and
`DK2.Simulation.Systems.ManaSystem`; the imp half is `ImpSupplyRules` /
`ImpSupplySystem` and its home is `imps-and-mana.md`.
**Tests:** `ManaTests` (`DK2.Simulation.Tests`) and `ManaOnRealDataTests`
(`DK2.Behavior.Tests`, skipped with no install).
**Scenario:** `scenarios/mana/land_pays_a_keeper.yaml`.

**Mana income is territory, plus a flat thirty for your heart.** Three terrain
types out of 41 generate any mana at all, and between them they are every kind of
ground a keeper can hold without having built on it. So the income rule is a sum
over owned tiles and one boolean.

Two things the files could not settle — the ceiling, and how to count a heart —
were answered by the owner from playing the original.

## Mana income is territory, and the data says so by exhaustion

The interesting thing about the terrain table is not the three numbers in it. It
is the 38 zeroes.

Claimed Path (1), Reinforced Wall (1) and Claimed Vault (100) are the only
terrains that pay anything, and they are — checked exhaustively against the
install by `ManaOnRealDataTests.OnlyHeldGroundGeneratesMana` — precisely the
terrains that are `Ownable` and are not a `Room`. Floor, wall and vault: the
three shapes a keeper's plain territory can take.

So the rule needs no policy of its own. A keeper's income is

```
min(  Σ over tiles they own of terrain.ManaGain
      + (30 if they have a dungeon heart)          ,  MaximumManaGainPerSecond  )
```

and three behaviours the original has fall out of the land term without being
written:

- **Claiming raises income**, because claiming is what turns Dirt Path into
  Claimed Path, and it is the *tile rules* that do that. `ManaRules` has never
  heard of claiming.
- **Losing land loses the income with it**, immediately, because the sum is over
  what is owned right now rather than over a running total.
- **Building a room on your own floor does not cost you income** — a correction
  (ADR-0334). Room terrain pays zero, but DK2 keeps the rate as a running word,
  `player+0x10E2`, moved by two routines: `Tile_SetOwner` (`0x004559D0`) hands
  the tile's *current* terrain's `ManaGain` from the old owner to the new, and
  `Tile_SetTerrain`'s adjustment (`0x00455B90`) swaps the old terrain's gain for
  the new one's only when **neither is a Room**. A room laid over claimed floor
  therefore keeps the floor's 1 on the books; a room the map laid, the heart and
  the portal pay 0 (they were owned with their room terrain); a room taken by a
  rival hands the rival 0, and the old owner goes on paying for it. The Level 3
  capture's word reads `claimed + reinforced + every room tile the keeper built`
  less its upkeep, to the tile, with the heart's 25 and the portal's 9 at 0. A
  bridge laid over water or lava adds 1 - to the tile's tag at that moment,
  which is the neutral player's, so no keeper gains it; its removal debits the
  builder. Ours keeps the floor's gain on the tile (`TileState.KeptManaGain`)
  and clears it when the tile changes hands; the old owner's leftover, the
  bridge's neutral 1 and a map-laid or captured room's 0 after it is sold are
  not mirrored (Q521).
- **The word carries the upkeep too.** The imp and trap registrations
  (`0x004C1AE0`–`0x004C1B90`) take their upkeep off `+0x10E2` as they put it on
  the drain, which is why the once-a-second payment adds the upkeep back before
  the cap and takes it off after (`0x004C10E0`): the cap sits over the gross.

The mana scenario (`scenarios/mana/land_pays_a_keeper.yaml`) pins the 38/s
before its treasury; the free imps' claims move the later rate, so the kept
floor is pinned by `ManaTests` rather than by the scenario.

### The dungeon heart's thirty

`DungeonHeartManaGenerationIncreasePerSecond` is 30 and it is per *heart*. That
phrasing was the whole difficulty, because a shipped heart covers 25 tiles and
`ManaRules` walks tiles rather than rooms — so "per heart" looked unimplementable
without inventing a way to count hearts.

It is not, because of a fact from the original rather than from the files:
**a keeper has at most one dungeon heart, ever, and losing it is defeat**
(CONFIRMED — owner observation; Q40). Given that, "per heart" and "per keeper who
has one" are the same quantity, and the count collapses to a boolean:
`ManaRules.GainPerSecondByPlayerIndex` sets a flag when it sees a heart tile and
adds 30 once. A 5×5 heart is worth 30 a second, not 750.

The ROOMS record agrees from its own side. The Dungeon Heart is the one room a
keeper starts with that carries no `Buildable` flag, so there is no way to
acquire a second — asserted against the install in
`ADungeonHeartCannotBeBuilt_WhichIsWhyAKeeperOnlyEverHasOne`.

Note what the 30 is *not*: heart terrain has a `ManaGain` of zero like every
other room terrain, so the 30 belongs to the keeper and not to the tiles. A heart
is laid by the map, never built, so its 25 tiles never kept a floor's 1 (ADR-0334).

### Why per second

`TerrainDef.ManaGain` carries no unit, which is the same ambiguity as Q19 for
the health deltas. Two things settle it well enough to call STRONGLY INFERRED:

1. `MaximumManaGainPerSecond` caps the total. A cap and the thing it caps have
   to be in the same unit.
2. The magnitudes agree. 500 against one per claimed tile puts the cap at 500
   claimed tiles — a large dungeon but a reachable one, and smaller than a
   shipped campaign map (asserted in `TheGainCapIsReachableOnAShippedMap`). Read
   per *tick* instead and the cap would sit somewhere no player could ever get
   to, which is not what a balance constant is for.

## The ceiling, and why it is a clamp

Read 2026-10-04 (Q39, Q76, QNEW-mana). CONFIRMED from the binary unless marked.

- **Payment is in whole seconds.** `Mana_UpdatePlayer` `0x004C10E0` (only caller
  `0x004BB586`) does nothing until `0x006F2590` (4) turns have passed since `player+0x9A`.
  Then it adds the whole second's net to mana `+0x82` in one step. The re-stamp of `+0x9A` is
  at `0x004C16B1`, and two exits after the payment skip it (`0x004C13F5`, the possession
  shortfall, and `0x004C1550`). On those turns the next turn pays again. Both are rare, so
  payment is once a second in ordinary play.
- **The ceiling is `player+0x10EA` and it is flat.** `Player_Initialise` (`0x004B8894`) and
  `0x004B9250` (`0x004B9271`) zero it. Its only other writer, `0x004C1A10`, adds its
  argument. Its only call (`0x0050BA84`, in `Level_InitTriggersAndStart`'s per-player loop)
  passes `MaximumManaThreshold` (`0x006F29CC`, whose only reader is `0x0050BA74`) minus the
  current value. That sets 200000 for each player at level start, however many hearts there
  are. It matches the owner's observation (Q37, Q37a).
- **The ceiling is a clamp, not a drain.** In `0x004C10E0` (`0x004C11C1`–`0x004C11E7`), a
  second whose net is not negative stores the ceiling when mana exceeds it. A negative net
  only floors mana at 0. `ExcessManaDecreaseRatePerSecond` (`0x006F25F5`) has no reference
  but its loader store (`0x0051CEF5`) in a whole-file absolute-address scan; a
  table-relative reader is not excluded. So mana above 200000 is cut back in one step at the
  next non-negative second. It does not drain at 5 a second. The 5/s drain this document
  inferred has no support in the binary. Built as ADR-0357 (`ManaRules.ApplyOneSecond`);
  whether anything else uses the variable is Q545's open half.

`DungeonHeartMaxManaIncrease` (32000) and `TerrainDef.MaxManaGain` (100, on
Claimed Vault alone) both look exactly like contributions to a computed
per-keeper ceiling. **Trap:** that reading is tidier than the truth — it gives
both variables a job and explains why 32000 is in the table at all — and it is
refuted, in both the strong form (a ceiling of 32000 a heart plus `MaxManaGain` a
tile, clamped at 200000) and the weak one (a ceiling that starts lower and grows
toward 200000, the observation being of a mature dungeon). The ceiling is 200000
from the first second and never moves (Q37a, owner observation; the binary agrees,
above). **Neither variable contributes to a mana ceiling.** `MaxManaGain` raises
the income cap `+0x10DE` (ADR-0334). `DungeonHeartMaxManaIncrease` has no reference
but its loader store. A table-relative load is not excluded, so "read by nothing" is
STRONGLY INFERRED.

That leaves `DungeonHeartMaxManaIncrease` with no candidate explanation: legacy data,
editor-only data, or something outside mana entirely. Q37a is closed on the
prohibition rather than solved. A normal outcome for a twenty-five-year-old balance table, recorded
because an unexplained number is exactly what a future session will find, decide
must mean something, and wire up.

## What spends it

**Imps do, and that half is built.** `imps-and-mana.md` is the home; in outline:

- **Upkeep, 7 a second per imp** (CONFIRMED — owner, 2026-08-30, Q53), read off
  the Create Imp *keeper-spell* record's `ManaUpkeepPerSecond` rather than
  CREATURES. `ManaRules.UpkeepPerSecondByPlayerIndex` charges it for **every**
  worker a keeper owns, including the free starting four, which is why the floor
  is free to fill and not free to keep.
- **Create Imp costs `1500 × (imps − MinimumImpThreshold + 1)`** — 1500, then
  3000, then 4500 (CONFIRMED, Q54). `ImpSupplySystem` refuses the command when
  mana is short and otherwise subtracts it.

**The two halves are capped and netted separately, and both orders are
observed.** `MaximumManaGainPerSecond` caps the *income* alone — netting first
would let a large workforce raise a keeper's income cap — while the ceiling is
applied to the net: the owner's screenshot of a full keeper reads exactly 200000
with +400 and −297 on the orb, so income beating upkeep pins them at the top
rather than 297 short. `ManaRules.ApplyOneSecond` does it in that order.

**Mana floors at zero**, and a keeper that cannot pay is warned four times on
the ten-second clock of `InsufficientManaImpEvaluationPeriodSeconds`, then loses
a creature every scan while it holds more than `MinimumImpThreshold` evil
workers: `imp-mana-removal.md`, built as ADR-0277 and STRONGLY INFERRED from
the binary alone (Q57 is the missing original run).

## What is not implemented, and is not guessed at

Read this before adding to `ManaRules`.

- **Spells other than Create Imp, Thunderbolt, Call to Arms, Heal, Inferno and Chicken.** Possess, Summon
  Horny and research/possession upkeep (`ManaCostInFirstPersonPerSecond`) all
  spend mana in DK2 and none of them exists here; the same spell column that gave
  the imp its 7 holds 2000 a second for Summon Horny. Call To Arms' 90 is charged
  after its prepaid period (`call-to-arms.md`, ADR-0279). Trap drain is owner-reported and uninvestigated.
- **A heart's fall is built** (ADR-0162; `win-and-defeat.md` §7): the verdict, the ruin,
  the defeated keeper's workers killed and the rest walked to a portal (Q40). Not built
  there: alliances ending games together (Q359).
- **`MaxManaGain` and `DungeonHeartMaxManaIncrease` are read by nothing.**
  `MaxManaGain` is non-zero on exactly one terrain in the whole table, where it
  repeats `ManaGain`; `DungeonHeartMaxManaIncrease` is 32000 against a flat
  200000 ceiling. `MaxManaGainIsSetOnExactlyOneTerrain_-`
  `WhichIsWhyNothingReadsIt` pins the negative result so that a future data set
  which contradicts it fails loudly.
- **Mana-vault claiming is not special-cased by its two named rates** (Q69,
  binary, 2026-09-07). `ClaimNeutralManaVaultHealth` and
  `ClaimEnemyManaVaultHealth` are loaded into globals after conversion to
  per-tick values, but neither global is ever read: both variables were CUT.
  The ordinary neutral-floor and hostile-floor work predicates admit the vault
  terrain pair, so their generic rates govern it. The mana rules pay for a
  claimed vault the moment claiming produces one.
- **No low-mana warning.** `LowManaWarningThreshold` (100) is interface, not
  simulation.

## How this was established

Cheapest first, per the method in `HANDOFF.md`. No disassembly was needed and
none was done.

1. `dk2tools kwd terrainmana` (added for this) over the install's `Terrain.kwd` —
   the whole 41-row table, both mana columns: 38 zeroes and three ownable
   terrains.
2. `dk2tools kwd vars` for the nine mana globals, cross-read against the field
   names for units.
3. Structural check rather than trust: `OnlyHeldGroundGeneratesMana` asserts the
   partition by exhaustion against the real database rather than spot-checking
   three ids, and `NoRoomGeneratesMana` walks all 26 rooms.
4. **Two facts came from the owner playing the game** and neither was reachable
   from the files: the ceiling is a flat 200000, and a keeper has exactly one
   dungeon heart whose loss is defeat. The first closed Q37 outright; the second
   turned the heart's 30 a second from unimplementable into a boolean.

The transcription into `Dk2TerrainFixture` (the two vault terrains, the four
rates) is guarded by `ManaOnRealDataTests`, as the terrain, room and Imp
transcriptions are.

## The cap, and an observation that does not fit it (2026-09-06, Q199)

**Two owner observations, same day.** On a skirmish map the mana regen read
**+500** and would not climb. On a My Pet Dungeon level it read **+1400 and
more**.

The first is the model to the digit: `ManaRules` clamps income at
`MaximumManaGainPerSecond` = 500, and a skirmish keeper with more than 500 paying
tiles sees exactly that. **The owner confirms it was a clamp and not a tile
count** (same evening): the map had more than 500 claimable tiles and the rate
would not pass 500 — *"a hard limit"*. That upgrades the cap from a reading of
the variable's name to **CONFIRMED** for skirmish. **The second is a number the
model cannot produce**, so the model is wrong or incomplete for that mode, and
the question is where. **The pet-dungeon half is flagged for later at the
owner's request**: they are not sure of the tile count or of what fed the +1400,
so it is an observation to re-check (`observation-checklist.md` §5), not yet a
measurement.

**2026-09-11, and it splits the two clamps.** On the My Pet Dungeon level the
owner names *Liberty*, the orb reads **+1400** income with the total at exactly
**200000** — `MaximumManaThreshold` to the digit, on two screenshots taken
minutes apart. So in that mode the **threshold is applied and the income cap is
not**, which narrows candidate 1 below and rules out "the display is simply
wrong": a display that invented its rate would not also stop dead on the other
variable's value. What *Liberty* is on disk is unresolved — the string appears
nowhere in the install in ASCII or UTF-16, and level text may sit inside a WAD,
so the 66-file sweep below cannot yet be said to cover it.

**The obvious explanation was checked first and it is refuted.** ADR-0040 layers
each level's own `<name>Variables.kld` over the global table, and a per-level
override would have explained a per-level cap in one line. So on 2026-09-06 all
**66** shipped `*Variables.kld` files under `Data/editor/maps` were parsed with
`KwdVariables.Parse` and asked for `MaximumManaGainPerSecond` and
`MaximumManaThreshold`: **none carries either.** Every level — campaign, skirmish,
secret, the seven `mpd*` pet-dungeon levels — inherits 500 and 200000 from
`GlobalVariables.kwd`. The +1400 is not a VARIABLES value.

Candidates, none chosen, ranked by how cheap they are to rule out:

1. **The binary applies the cap to something other than the displayed rate, or
   not at all in the pet-dungeon mode.** Cheapest to settle: find the reader of
   variable 235 in `DKII.EXE` and see what it clamps and under what condition.
2. **The displayed "+N" includes a source that is not territory income.** A
   pet-dungeon level may add mana by some route the display folds into the rate.
   Settled by play: note the level, count claimed tiles, and watch whether the
   +1400 falls when land is lost and by how much per tile.
3. **The value sits in a record this project does not decode** — PLAYERS(180)
   or LEVEL(220), both split and unread.
4. **`MaxManaGain` (100) and `DungeonHeartMaxManaIncrease` (32000)**, the two
   orphan numbers above. Ruled out for the *ceiling*; not ruled out for the
   *rate*.

Until one of those lands, the confidence line for the cap reads: **CONFIRMED for
skirmish (owner), CONTRADICTED for My Pet Dungeon (owner), mechanism UNKNOWN.**
`ManaRules` is not changed on an observation of a mode this project does not
run; it is flagged.
